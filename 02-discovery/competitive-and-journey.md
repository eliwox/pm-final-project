# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A consumer on shift or variable income, with one or more open claims, trying to settle what she owes through the digital portal.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** To settle what she owes with a plan she can afford and keep, without making her situation worse.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** At the income question, the journey asks for her information without explaining itself. She can’t tell whether “income” means this month or a normal month (N-01), nothing says why it’s asked or how it’s used (T-206), and she fears a wrong number could be used against her (N-06). She has no way to see what happens if she misses a payment, so she defaults to the plan that “looked like the right answer” and misses the second payment (N-02), or she closes the portal and doesn’t come back (N-01). With two claims, she also can’t tell whether paying one stops the letters about the other (N-03, T-202).
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** the research does show that she is pushed to non-digital or off-journey channels:

Phone call to an agent (N-04, N-08). This is the only confirmed outside channel.
Her own memory and guesswork, since she has no calculator or explainer in the journey (N-01, N-02).
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** The process (the confirmed path)
She opens the portal on a break, on her phone, and starts the instalment journey (N-01).
She reaches the income question and can’t tell whether it means this month or a normal month (N-01).
She leaves the question unanswered, either by closing the portal “to come back” (N-01) or by choosing the shortest plan as the “right answer” without checking affordability (N-02).
Unsure about consequences, she phones an agent to ask what happens if she misses a payment or whether it will cost more (N-04).
The agent asks her for the same details she already typed, because he can’t see them (N-08, T-205).
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The income question. It is where the journey asks for sensitive information without explaining which income is meant, why it’s needed or how it will be used (N-01, T-206). It is also where the fear of getting it wrong appears (N-06). Everything after it, such as guessing a plan, dropping out or calling, follows from that unanswered moment.

A second break point comes after she agrees: the missed second payment (N-02), when she discovers the plan was unaffordable.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** Quote (confusion): “It asked for my income and I did not know if they meant this month or a normal month. I closed it and meant to come back.” (N-01)
Quote (guessing): “I picked the plan with the lowest number of months because it looked like the right answer. The amount was too high and I missed the second payment.” (N-02)
Quote (fear): “I was scared to type the wrong number. What if they use it against me?” (N-06)
Quote (calling): “A person can tell me if this is going to cost me more.” (N-04)
Behavior in the data: “Why income is asked” is 16.2% of agent contacts and “what if I miss a payment” is 18.6%. Variable or shift earners miss payments at 22.5% versus 17.4% for fixed salary.
